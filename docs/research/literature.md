# Literature survey: belief-state move selection for Fog of War chess (2026-11-18)

Sources were fetched and read (PDF text or source code), not recalled. Working copies: `scratchpad/research/pdfs/*.txt`, `scratchpad/research/sf1/` (StrangeFish 2019 code), `scratchpad/research/sf2/` (StrangeFish2 code).

## 1. RBC competition bots

**StrangeFish (2019 winner).** Code: https://github.com/ginop/reconchess-strangefish (`strategies/multiprocessing_strategies.py`, `utilities/rbc_move_score.py`, `config.yml`). Per (board, move) score: king capture = +50,000; moving into check = -40,000; otherwise Stockfish depth 8 centipawns of the resulting position minus the pre-move score (a delta), +300 if the move attacks the enemy king "sneakily" (non-capture or discovered). A requested move that is blocked on a board is replaced by the move the referee would actually execute (`simulate_move`), or by pass, and that is what gets scored. Board weight w(s) = 1/(1+exp(-2 ln3 · cp_opp(s)/P90)), P90 = 10,000 cp in the shipped config: boards where the opponent's last move produced a good position for them are more likely. Compound = 0.9·weighted mean + 0.1·min + 0·max; random pick among moves within 1% of the best. Time: remaining/20 turns (config; code default /7), min 1 s, 80% sensing, 15-20% moving; the number of boards sampled = time ÷ (measured seconds per Stockfish call × #moves). Verdict: we have the equivalent (mean + king-loss risk); the mean/min weights are the only knob and we found such knobs neutral.

**StrangeFish2 (2nd 2021, 1st 2022, Elo 1762).** Code: https://github.com/ginoperrotta/reconchess-strangefish2. Organizers attribute the 2022 win to "developments in board state value heuristics ... and prioritized sub-sampling of tracked positions" (Gardner et al. 2023, https://proceedings.mlr.press/v220/gardner23a/gardner23a.pdf). `rbc_move_score.py` defaults: capture_king 9,000 + material + 0.85·prev; checkmate 8,000 × 0.95^plies; into_check -7,000, extra -2,000 for remaining in check; opponent-into-check -4,000; Stockfish depth 7; score = 0.85·absolute + 0.15·delta; then additive heuristics in cp: **own undefended material × 0.1 (`hanging_material_penalty_ratio`), weak squares around own king (open lines to it given which enemy piece types still exist) -1 each**, sneaky king attack +20 (-15 if the king can escape by capturing), quiet check threats +15 each, supported pawn pressure +15 / unsupported +5, passed pawn +15, castling rights +15, capture -5 (reveals position; RBC-specific), pass +5. Move choice: compound = 0.7·weighted mean + 0.3·min (`MoveConfig`), uniform random among moves within 10 cp of the best; if the set has one board, 90% of the time play Stockfish's 2 s move directly (capturing the king if legal). Weights: same sigmoid, P90 = 3,000 cp, floor 0.02. Sub-sampling: a UCT-style loop picks which (move, board) pair to evaluate next, boards drawn by a priority assigned when the set was expanded, stopping when the top move is stable for 100 iterations; `SENSE_SAMPLE_LIMIT` 2,500, `SCORE_SAMPLE_LIMIT` 250. Time: remaining/16, clamped to [3, 40] s, 30% of it for the move; under 60 s left a "last ditch" switch to mean-only, threshold ×10, king-attack rewards ×10. Per-opponent opening books (`opening_books/opening_W_<opponent>.pk`, default `_ALL`) and hand tweaks for trout/attacker/random. No published ablation of the heuristics; tournament result only. Verdict: the material heuristics (hanging material, weak squares, unsafe attacker) are the single thing we lack that maps onto our loss mode.

**Fianchetto (1st 2021, Elo 1759, 91% wins; 2nd 2022).** Taufeeque, Tongia, Kalyanakrishnan, "Fianchetto: Speed, Belief, Guile, Caution", https://taufeeque9.github.io/assets/pdf/bachelors_thesis.pdf (the "Bernardoni" attribution is wrong; Bernardoni was a 2019 organizer/entrant). Built on StrangeFish; Lc0 policy network replaces Stockfish. Ablation (Table 1, 60-game matches vs StrangeFish as column 1): V1 (Lc0 only) slightly worse than V0; V2 persistent Bayesian belief b' ∝ P(z|s',a)·Σ_s P(s'|s,a)P(a|s)b(s) with P(a|s) = Lc0 policy → 39-21; V3 risk-graded incentives → 48-12; V4 → 48-12 with gains elsewhere. Move = argmax_a Σ_s P(a|s)P(s|b) (belief-weighted policy vote, not value). V3 "caution": sneak-check bonus scaled by the moved piece's value and whether it is attacked; pawn attacks rewarded only if the pawn is supported; "faraway defense" (block a probable check with a supported piece rather than move the king, because the opponent will sense/strike the king square). V4: belief = λ·Bayes + (1-λ)·uniform with λ = 0.8 / 0.5 / 0 for |I| < 5k / < 20k / > 20k. 2022 version added opponent-specific defensive weights and a closed-position opening book. Verdict: we have Bayesian weighting; size-dependent λ and risk-graded sneak bonuses are cheap adds.

**Kevin (4th 2021, 3rd 2022).** Only the tournament reports describe it (https://proceedings.mlr.press/v176/perrotta22a/perrotta22a.pdf; Gardner 2023): distribution over full histories; 2021: depth-limited CFR with unsafe subgame solving on a sampled board subset to infer the opponent's distribution; 2022: ResNet+LSTM trained by imitation on StrangeFish2 logs predicts opponent senses and moves; move = max expected Stockfish value. No code or write-up found. Verdict: evidence that a learned opponent model lands 3rd, behind hand-tuned Stockfish heuristics.

**Oracle (3rd 2019, 5th 2021, 6th 2022).** Plurality vote of Stockfish's best move across all boards (uniform). No author write-up found. **ROOKie (5th 2022):** each board votes for its top-5 moves, against moves leaving the king in check. **wbernar5 (5th 2019):** pure worst-case Stockfish over ≤500 boards. **JKU-CODA (8th 2022):** worst-case + weighted average. **trout:** single most-recent-observation board + Stockfish; beaten by roughly half the field every year. Verdict: voting and worst-case both rank below mean-with-king-penalty every year.

**Penumbra / Châteaux (Clark, NeurIPS 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.01810).** Learned synopsis network + UCT on sampled infostates; "caution" κ (fraction of playouts giving the opponent better information) and "paranoia" φ: argmax_a (1-φ)·q_a/n_a + φ·min_a + c·π_a·sqrt(ln n / n_a). Grid search (250 games/cell, Elo ±19): baseline 1711, best 1726 (κ 20%, φ 10%), worst 1666 (30/30): all noise or slightly negative. Also: "modeling the opponent with a stronger policy outperformed modeling it more accurately". Verdict: matches our neutral CVaR results; skip.

**Organizer analyses.** Board-set size and Stockfish top-3 agreement correlate with rank but StrangeFish2 ranked 10th of 15 on set size and 6th on agreement while winning (Gardner 2023, Table 3). 2021 opening stats (RBC, not FoW): 1.e4 37% of games, 71% White wins; 1.d4 17%, 66% losses; 1.c4 5%, 83% wins.

**Scorca (Stöhr et al., ICAART 2024, https://www.scitepress.org/Papers/2024/123542/123542.pdf).** Lc0 rank penalty 4·log(idx+1) summed over boards; 10-game samples only. Not evidence.

## 2. Dark chess / Kriegspiel

**Zhang & Sandholm 2021** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.06068): 1-KLSS agent vs baseline (same agent, depth-1 imperfect-info search + Stockfish): 59.5-40.5 over 100 games; lost 1-9 to FM Luis Chan (2416); "often threw the advantage away in the endgame by playing too pessimistically".

**Obscuro (Zhang & Sandholm, ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.01242).** Stockfish 14 MultiPV **depth 1** at leaves, clamped to [-1,1]; 1 CFR thread + 2 expansion threads; avg 17,264 possible positions, 1.07M nodes, 10.7 plies deep at 5 s/move on 5 cores. 85.1% vs ZS21 (1,000 games); 80% vs the #1 human (2318). Ablations at 5 s/move, full vs ablated: purification off (mix among all stable actions instead of ≤3 and only when the strategy is safe) 70.2%; KLUSS off 58.0%; one-sided GT-CFR off 53.3%; non-uniform Resolve root off 53.3%; material+visibility eval instead of Stockfish 81.9% (yet that crippled version still beats ZS21 55%). **Time scaling, 10k games each: 1/8→1/4 s 56.4%, 1/4→1/2 56.5%, 1/2→1 56.7%, 1→2 54.0%, 2→4 53.7%, 4→8 52.3%, 8→16 52.4%** (≈ +45 Elo per doubling under 1 s, +16 Elo per doubling above 4 s). Opening distribution (Table 1): White d4 66%, c4 30%, e4 2%; Black Nc6 32%, c6 25%, e6 21%, Nf6 16%, d5 0.9% — the a4-e8 diagonal (Qa4+/Qa5+) decides every ≤12-ply game. White scores 57.5% in self-play. Appendix E.7: "most games ... are decided by one side outright blundering material because of lack of knowledge ... more skilled players ... restrict the probability of losing material". Obscuro does not model the opponent. Verdict: we tested PCFR+ subgame solving as neutral; the transferable items are the time curve, the opening table, and purified mixing.

**Misty** (https://github.com/brianhliou/misty): Obscuro reimplementation; its two tracked "strength" defects are losing the true board past the enumeration cap and failing to convert won endgames vs a bare king. No numbers.

**Ciancarini & Favini, IJCAI 2009** (https://www.ijcai.org/Proceedings/09/Papers/086.pdf): "approach C" = 1-ply UCT whose value is material minus a hand-coded hazard model (P(move illegal) = Σ occupancy probabilities on the path; 99% that a capturing piece is recaptured, halving per retaliation; 30% that a checking piece is lost; 10% baseline loss) beat Darkboard (minimax, ~1700 ICC) by >100 Elo, 3:1 wins/losses, with no other knowledge. Nothing substantive on Kriegspiel after 2010 was found.

## 3. Paranoid vs expectation

Parker, Nau, Subrahmanian, AAAI 2006 (https://www.cs.umd.edu/~nau/papers/parker2006overconfidence.pdf): information-set minimax ("paranoid") vs uniform-random opponent ("overconfident") in Kriegspiel, 500 games/cell: vs random, overconfident depth 2 wins 50%/loses 1% vs paranoid 29%/3%; head-to-head overconfident wins 15-31% vs 2-5% losses; vs their HS player overconfident depth 1 +14 EU vs paranoid at best +3. Reason given: "if that move is a loss on just one history ... it will not be chosen". More samples helped the expectation player; deeper search without more time hurt both. Together with Penumbra's grid and wbernar5/JKU-CODA placements, the literature agrees with our tests: risk posture is not the lever; per-state pricing of material loss is.

Using the opponent's blindness: Fianchetto V3, StrangeFish2 `require_sneak`, Obscuro's bluffs (Bxd6 when Black cannot see whether d6 is covered) are the only published forms. None is quantified separately except Fianchetto V3 (+9 games/60 vs StrangeFish, pooled with pawn-attack incentives).

## 4. Time management

Evidence: Obscuro's scaling table above; Parker 2006 (samples matter more than depth); Ciancarini (k=1 wins at 1 s, longer plans only pay at 4 s). Practice: StrangeFish remaining/20-30; StrangeFish2 remaining/16 clamped [3, 40] s with an aggressive mode under 60 s; Penumbra time ∝ remaining; Obscuro "heuristic on remaining clock". No one publishes a per-move allocation ablation. Our remaining/30 + 0.8·inc spends 7.6 s on move 1 of a 180+2 game and never drains; the top bot drains to 12 s.

## Top 5 missing items, by expected value

1. **Material hazard pricing from the state set.** Add to each candidate's value an expected immediate material loss computed over the full set (not the 64 roots): for each state, SEE of the opponent's best capture after our move, weighted by our existing state weights, optionally up-weighted when the capturing piece is outside the opponent's vision in that state (we can compute both sides' vision per state). StrangeFish2's `hanging_material_penalty_ratio`/`weak_squares`, Fianchetto V3, Ciancarini's hazard model and Obscuro E.7 all point here, and 24/28 of our losses are material. Cheap (exact set already exists; SEE is in python-chess-adjacent code).
2. **Clock: drain to a reserve.** e.g. budget = (remaining − 12)/max(10, est_moves_left) + 0.9·inc, clamped like StrangeFish2. Obscuro's curve says our sub-second-to-few-second regime is where each doubling is worth 20-45 Elo. Trivial to implement and to measure.
3. **Opening book.** Seed from Obscuro Table 1 (1.d4/1.c4; as Black cover a4-e8 with ...c6/...Nc6/...e6, never ...d5 vs c4) and prefer closed positions (Fianchetto 2022). Removes the fog-tax of early Stockfish calls and the two-move Qa4+ losses. Cheap.
4. **Zero-risk-first plus purified randomness.** Lexicographic: discard any move that loses the king in any state of the full set when a zero-risk alternative exists (StrangeFish2's -7,000 × 0.3 min weight does this de facto), then pick uniformly among moves within ~10 cp (StrangeFish2) but from at most 3 (Obscuro purification: 70.2% vs unlimited mixing). The top arena bot has both. Cheap.
5. **Belief hygiene from Fianchetto V4 / StrangeFish2:** make the 80/20 model-vs-uniform mix depend on set size (0.8/0.5/0 at 5k/20k), and when the set has exactly one state play Stockfish's move at full depth with the whole budget. Modest evidence, nearly free.
